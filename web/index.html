What happens when you advance

  1. The forecast is written down first. The lab records, for every pool, the forecast and the plan as they stand, so there is something to hold the future against.
  2. The pools get new readings. Each pool follows its own recent trend, with a growth surprise the forecast cannot see, and noise as large as its own history. On top of that, the requests, contracts and events that fall due become usage, or do not: a request goes live with the likelihood it was given, and only part of what was asked for shows up. A pool cannot use more than has landed, so a full pool turns demand away.
  3. Supply arrives, or slips. An approved order is placed on its date. An order that reaches its planned day lands, or slips, by however far the vendor's own delivery history says arrivals stray from the typical one.
  4. Then you see the difference: the demand the plan counted against the actual, what fell due and what became of it, what arrived when, and how each plan changed because time passed.

The readings are generated, so this shows how the loop works, not how accurate the forecast would be on real usage. Try approving East US's order on its pool page first, then come back and advance.
