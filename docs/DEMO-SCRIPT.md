# Five-minute demo script

Generated by `npm run docs` from the engine and the shipped baseline data, so every number below is what the screen shows. `npm test` fails if this file drifts. Start clean with `npm run demo`.

The story in one line: a product team asks for capacity; the system says how much fits now, what to order, when, what it costs and why; a named planner decides; the decision is recorded.

## 1. The answer first (Capacity Planning, Balance / plan, 90 seconds)

- Read the headline aloud: "No. 1 of 6 pools is already past the date an order should have been placed, and 2 more need an order now." The three chips under it answer the question directly: next 3 months **Act**, 6 months **At risk**, 12 months **At risk**.
- The six KPI cards, each with an (i) that says how the number is calculated: total demand 39,334 CU at 12 months (+12% on last quarter; the pools are combined as one forecast, so this is 904 CU below the 40,238 CU that adding each pool's p80 would give); projected shortfall 7,294 CU (19% of demand); 3 of 4 regions on watch or at risk; a projected reclaim value of $645K (2,231 CU) that needs no purchase; an estimated investment of $7.43M to resolve the critical gaps; and 11 critical signals across 6 funnels.
- Capacity health by region, projected utilization at 12 months: North America 128% (at risk), Europe 73% (healthy), Asia Pacific 81% (watch), Latin America 122% (at risk). The regions are dots on a world map, each where its pools are (weighted by capacity), with the label carrying the figures; selecting one filters the page.
- The forecast chart plots demand against provisioned and effective capacity, with shortfall bars. The call-out reads "Projected shortfall 7,294 CU". The 3, 6 and 12 month tabs redraw it; hover a month for all four values.
- Under the cards, **Supply against demand, by pool** is the balance itself. At 12 months demand is 39,334 CU against 43,104 CU of supply (36,153 CU at the working ceilings), and 4 of 6 pools are short. Each bar shows the pool's supply, its working ceiling (the tick) and the demand we plan for (the marker). East US (FAB-INTEL-ICX-64) is furthest out, by 4,650 CU, and its plan is to order 4,032 CU by 10 Aug 2026. Balance is per pool: the 3,210 CU of room elsewhere cannot cover it.
- Point at the first recommendation: Procure 4,032 CU of FAB-AMD-GENOA-96 for East US. It says what kind of planning set it: **standard demand-driven planning**, or **funnel-triggered** when another funnel overrides it. Then the constraints table and the top signals (28 in all). The inventory and the optimization opportunities are on the Supply view.
- The Action Queue holds 13 actions, ranked P0 first: 5 procurement, 5 allocation, 3 other. The header Actions button opens it. The SKU and region filters rescope everything on the page, including the headline; the horizon filter changes the chart and the region health.
- Scroll to "Planning horizon by pool", which lists every pool by urgency with its exhaustion date, order and lead time. The first row: East US (FAB-INTEL-ICX-64) is **OVERDUE**. Order 4,032 CU of FAB-AMD-GENOA-96 today (42 days overdue).

## 2. Supply and demand (Capacity Planning, the Supply view and the Demand view, 90 seconds)

- Capacity Planning is one page with three views: **Balance / plan** (where you are), **Supply view** and **Demand view**. They replace the two pages a provider and a requester used to see separately, so the two sides of the plan cannot disagree. One set of filters (horizon, SKUs, regions) applies to all three, and the old Overview and Product Team addresses still open the matching view.
- Click **Supply view**. Six figures, each with an (i): capacity available 9,678 CU; pool capacity 41,088 CU; utilization 59%; headroom 10,089 CU; health 3 of 6 pools; cost $34.22M. Below them, every pool with its installed capacity, what lands, utilization, headroom, health and value; then the inventory by SKU and the optimization opportunities: reclaim 1,196 CU in West Europe and reclaim 1,035 CU in East US.
- Click **Demand view**. Six figures: product demand 39,334 CU; workload forecast 33,623 CU; growth +176 CU a week; pipeline 2,018 CU; business events 9; capacity requests 9. Product demand is the same number as the balance's total demand. Below them, demand by pool, and the 9 business events dated within 12 months: contracts, sized launches and relocations, seasonal spikes, and signals that inform the plan without adding demand.
- Change the horizon to 3 months on any view: the figures, the balance and the events all move to 3 months together.

## 3. Why now (East US pool, 90 seconds)

## 4. Change one assumption (What if, 30 seconds)

- In the What-if panel set the lead time to 8 weeks. The state changes from OVERDUE to **PLAN** and the raise-by date moves from 10 Aug 2026 to 2 Nov 2026. Nothing is saved. The point: most of the urgency is lead time, not demand.

## 6. The seven layers (Ontology, 60 seconds)

- Open Ontology. Every pool is described in the same seven layers, and each row answers one question. Four layers are **recorded** (read straight from a data file) and three are **derived** (worked out from the others).
- Read down the East US column: 9,600 CU installed, 9,340 CU reserved by teams, 77% in use, 1,035 CU that could be given back, an end-of-life SKU, a health score of 3.5, and a plan that is **OVERDUE**. That is one pool told in seven layers, and the last row is the plan you have already seen.
- Now Southeast Asia: only 56% in use, but a health score of 35.0 against a critical line of 24 (worst segment sea-fabric-2). The health layer is why utilization alone is not the story.
- Select that pool. Seven cards give every value behind the row, the file it comes from and the funnels that read it. The gap and waste card shows 269 CU of idle reservation but counts 0 CU as an opportunity, because it is below the flag threshold. That is why the reclaim total on Capacity Planning still adds up.
- The values are live: apply a scenario in the Lab and this page changes with it.

## 8. Is the plan safe at the busiest hour? (Peak check, 60 seconds)

- The plan is sized on weekly means, but capacity has to cover the busiest hour of the week. On the Balance / plan view, scroll to "Is the plan safe at the busiest hour?".
- On the weekly mean no pool is over the working ceiling. At the busiest hour 1 of 6 is. On its busiest hour East US runs at 89% of capacity, above the 85% working ceiling (5 weeks running), while its weekly mean is 77%. Planned on the busiest hour the order is 5,952 CU instead of 4,032 (1,920 CU more, $595K more), needed 14 weeks sooner.
- If the working ceiling is meant for the busiest hour, 2 plans change state (Japan East: PLAN to OVERDUE, Brazil South: PLAN to OVERDUE), and the estate would order 3,456 CU more, costing $2.62M more, than the weekly plan asks for.
- Open East US: the ladder shows the weekly mean, the busiest hour, the working ceiling and the hourly model's forecast for next week on one scale, and the table sets the two plans side by side.
- Nothing on any plan changed. Whether the working ceiling applies to the busiest hour is a policy decision for planners; the check shows what each answer costs. The ratio and the forecast come from the generated hourly corpus in `ml/`.

## 9. The decision (60 seconds)

- On the East US pool, enter a name. Try approving 3,840 CU without a reason: the system refuses and says why. Add a reason and approve. The order ORD-LAB-0001 enters the supply pipeline and the pool shows it in flight.
- Try the name `engine`: the engine that drafted the plan cannot approve it.
- Open Decisions: the record is hash-chained, and the log integrity reads Verified. Each record also keeps what it was based on: the dataset, the forecast (with a fingerprint of the exact history and settings) and a fingerprint of the plan, because the engine refits on every load and would otherwise leave nothing to check a decision against.

## 10. What can be done, and who has to agree (Options and approval, 90 seconds)

- On the East US pool, above the Decision panel, is **Options**. 2 options are offered: **Order as drafted** and **Wait 4 weeks**. Phasing is not offered: the second order, 960 CU, would already have been due on 10 Aug 2026. This pool is late, so ordering less now only adds a second late order. Each option that places an order is measured by running the plan again with that order in flight, so what it says is what approving it produces. The rule that recommends one is on the page: Order what closes the gap if the budget allows it. If it does not, order the most whole racks the budget allows and say what is left. Never wait: waiting is shown so its cost can be seen, not to be chosen.
- Open West Europe (FAB-GPU-H100-8). The options are **Order as drafted (1,344 CU, $5.64M)**, **Order 1,056 CU now, size the rest later (1,056 CU, $4.44M)**, **Wait 4 weeks (nothing)**. The Decision panel already says what approving needs: An order of $5.64M is above $1.50M, so a second, different named person has to countersign it. No order is placed until they do.
- Approve as Asha Rao. No order is placed: Asha Rao's approval of 1,344 CU is waiting, the header says so, and the plan still shows the need. Enter the same name again and it is refused; enter Ben Ortiz and click **Countersign**, and only then does the order enter the supply pipeline. Declining or deferring withdraws a waiting approval. The lab has no sign-in, so a named person is a name that was typed: this is a control on the record and the workflow, not authentication.
- The budget is synthetic (FY27 Q2 capacity budget, $8.00M), and so are the thresholds. With East US and the H100 order approved, $6.84M is committed. Open Japan East (FAB-GPU-L40S-8): the order as drafted (2,592 CU, $3.50M) is $2.33M over the budget, so the recommendation is now **Order what the budget allows**: 768 CU for $1.04M, the most whole racks that fit, leaving 1,728 CU still needed by 23 Nov 2026. **Use this option** fills in the Decision panel. Going over the budget is allowed, on a written reason.
- **Explain this plan** is now given more to work with, all of it produced by the engine, the planner or the rules above: what changed since the last decision or since the lab moved, the options and which one the rule recommends, what approving needs, the budget, what waiting costs, and why this priority. It explains; it never ranks or chooses. The ranking is fixed by the engine, and the advisor is told so: "Priority P2 (medium). Of 3 open orders this ranks 3rd, by state (PLAN), then priority, then how soon the order has to be raised. The ranking is fixed by the engine." Every figure in its text is still checked against the plan.

## 11. What happened next (Outcome and feedback, 90 seconds)

- On the rail open **Outcome**. Today in the lab is 21 Sep 2026, and the lab can move 26 weeks: 26 weeks is the most this lab generates. Contracts, events and requests that fall due inside them are realized in the actuals.
- Click **Advance 13 weeks**, then **Advance 4 weeks**. Before the world moves the lab writes down, for every pool, the forecast, the demand the plan counted and the plan as they stand. That record is what the future is held against. Then each pool gets new readings, the requests, contracts and events that fall due become usage (or do not), and supply arrives or slips.
- The readings are generated: each pool follows its own recent trend with a hidden growth surprise (-25% to +35% against its fitted trend) and noise as large as its own history. The forecast never sees the surprise, so it can be wrong. On top of the trend, whatever falls due becomes usage, or does not (below). The loop is real; the accuracy is not evidence about real usage.
- **Planned demand against actual.** The plan's demand is the forecast with the pipeline and the dated adds on top: what the orders were sized for. 80 of 102 weekly readings were at or under its p80 (78%), with an average error of 1.47%; the trend model on its own, held against the trend and the steps already in its history, was off by 1.12%. East US was 0.6% off on average but above p80 in 10 of 17 weeks: its band was too narrow. That is the red triangles on its chart. Each week is held against the plan recorded most recently before it, not one refitted afterwards.
- **What fell due.** 10 requests, contracts and events fell due and 8 happened. The plan counted 2,054 CU of them; 2,015 CU showed up. REQ-1001 asked for 480 CU; the plan counted 288 CU (its likelihood, less the overlap discount); it went live and 466 CU showed up. REQ-1002 (1,100 CU) did not go ahead: the plan had counted 578 CU of it. Whether each one happens is decided by draws the plan never sees, so the plan can be wrong. A settled item leaves the pipeline, because its usage is in the readings now. The **What fell due** card on the page lists every one.
- **Supply.** ORD-2026-0412 (East US) was due 9 Nov 2026 and landed 30 Nov 2026, 3 weeks late, adding 1,152 CU to the pool. How far an order strays comes from the vendor's own delivery history.
- **The approved order.** DEC-0001 was placed with the vendor on its date (status: ordered) and is not here yet. When it was written down, East US was **WATCH** because that order covered the need; it now reads **OVERDUE**, needing 1,728 CU more.
- **Plans, then and now.** 4 of 6 plans changed; 0 pools were overdue then and 3 are now. Where nothing was done, waiting shows as an order that lands later: Southeast Asia's now lands 21 Jun 2027, 17 weeks after 22 Feb 2027.
- The whole lab moves with it: every page shows the world as of the new date, an **Advanced** marker sits in the header, and the busiest-hour check steps aside (its profile was measured on the shipped history). Reset lab on the Lab page puts today back.

## What to say if asked

- **Is this Microsoft data?** No. Every record is generated and labelled `source: "synthetic"`; the loader refuses anything else.
- **Is it AI?** The demand forecast is a fitted model, backtested against a straight line. Every other funnel is a rule on a record, so every number is deterministic and traceable. There is no LLM in the decision path. An optional Azure OpenAI explainer re-tells the finished plan in plain words and drafts the request; it cannot change a date, quantity or SKU, and every figure in its text is checked against the plan.
- **Why 14 funnels?** They are the 14 in the Engineering Requirements. Each is independent and any one can flag a pool even when the others say it is healthy.
- **Why is the total lower than adding up the pools?** Each pool plans at p80. For a group to miss at its p80, its pools would have to miss on the high side together; pools whose errors do not move together partly cancel. The Overview combines the pools as one forecast, using how much their weekly movements go the same way (measured from the data, never taken below zero), so a region's or the total's p80 sits below the sum of its pools' p80s. In a simulated test the combined p80 covers about 80% of outcomes whether the pools' errors are unrelated or move together, while adding the p80s over-covers badly when they are unrelated. A single pool, and every pool's own plan, orders and shortfall, are unchanged.
- **Does the plan use the busiest hour?** No. It uses weekly means, as the requirements do, and the Overview says so. The peak check shows what the plan would ask for if the working ceiling were applied to the busiest hour. It appears only when `npm run ml` has built the peak profile, and its figures come from generated hourly data.
- **Where are the seven ontology layers?** On the Ontology page, with each layer's live value for every pool, the file it comes from and the funnels that read it. Four are recorded (infrastructure, allocation, utilization, SKU lifecycle) and three are derived (gap and waste, reliability and health, planning horizon).
- **The Overview follows the customer wireframe. What is different?** Every figure comes from this lab's own synthetic estate, not the mockup's. Four regions, not eight (the data has no Middle East pools). No confidence percentage: the engine does not produce one, so a recommendation says what kind of planning set it and how many funnels converge. "Effective capacity" is provisioned capacity times the working ceiling; there is no disaster-recovery figure. Inventory covers GPU and Compute only, with no Storage or Networking. The region map is a simple world outline with a dot per region, not a street map. There is no Methodology or Settings page, and no "last 12 weeks" filter.
- **The Product Team (Requester) view follows a wireframe too. What is different?** It is its own page, My Capacity Overview, built from the same records and the same request assessment as the planner. There is no AI confidence percentage: the recommendation is rules over the plan and says what it rests on. Cost is a one-off purchase cost, not the annual or monthly figure the mockup shows, because the lab has no operating-cost data. There is no "vs last month" change on current usage (no team usage history is kept), only demand against the forecast as it stood a quarter ago. Revenue at risk, customer commitment, SLA impact and strategic importance are what the requester wrote on the request, never estimated. Drivers, options and risks are derived from the plan and the funnels. Whether a workload can run in another region is not known, so the other-region options say so.
- **What would it take to run on real data?** Replace the 14 files in `data/` with extracts in the same shape (see docs/DATA.md). The engine, the API and the screens do not change.
